What a Local SEO Audit Actually Checks (2026 Complete Guide)

Quick Takeaways

A complete local SEO audit in 2026 checks 7 dimensions — not just your Google Business Profile. AI search visibility is now a core audit area. ChatGPT recommends only 1.2% of business locations, while Google's Local 3-Pack appears in 35.9% of searches. Service area businesses need different audit criteria than brick-and-mortar. Multi-location brands require a completely different framework. And most audit checklists you find online skip geo-grid analysis, voice search, and conversion metrics entirely.

Introduction

Most local SEO audits check four things: your Google Business Profile, your citations, your reviews, and your on-page keywords. That was a complete audit in 2021.

In 2026, it covers less than half the picture.

AI-powered search has fundamentally changed how local businesses get discovered. Google AI Overviews now appear in 68% of local searches. Forty-five percent of consumers use AI tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity for local recommendations. If your audit does not check how you appear inside these systems, you are auditing a version of local search that no longer exists.

This guide covers what a complete local SEO audit actually checks across all 7 dimensions — including the ones most agencies and tools still miss.

Chapter 1: The 7 Dimensions of a Complete Local SEO Audit

A thorough local SEO audit in 2026 covers seven areas. Traditional audits typically cover three or four. Here is what the full picture looks like:

1. Google Business Profile Signals — 25% of local pack ranking weight. The single most important local ranking factor.

2. NAP Consistency and Citations — Your business name, address, and phone number must match exactly across every directory. Inconsistencies dilute your local authority.

3. On-Page and Location Page Signals — Your website's local relevance signals: title tags, H1s, LocalBusiness schema, internal linking structure, and content uniqueness.

4. Review and Reputation Signals — Review count, velocity, rating, response rate, and platform distribution. Reviews now carry approximately 20% of local ranking weight, up from 16% in 2022.

5. AI Search Visibility — Whether ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Gemini recommend your business. Most audits do not check this at all.

6. Voice Search Readiness — Whether your content answers question-based queries in a format voice assistants can retrieve and read aloud.

7. Conversion and Engagement Metrics — Whether your local visibility actually converts to calls, direction requests, and website clicks. Rankings without conversions mean nothing.

Chapter 2: Google Business Profile Audit

The GBP audit is where most local SEO audits begin and end. It should be the foundation — not the whole structure.

GBP signals account for approximately 25% of local pack ranking weight. The audit checklist:

Primary Category — The single most impactful local pack factor. An incorrect primary category is also the single most damaging negative factor. Audit this first.

NAP Accuracy — Business name, address, and phone number must exactly match what appears on your website and across all directories. Even minor variations (St. vs Street, suite number formats, tracking phone numbers) create signal inconsistency.

Business Hours — Including special hours for holidays and seasonal changes. Outdated hours generate negative reviews and reduce Google's trust in your listing.

Photos and Videos — Quality, recency, and geo-tagged metadata. Listings with photos receive 42% more direction requests than those without.

Reviews and Response Rate — Both quantity and response quality matter. A 100% response rate — even to negative reviews — signals active business management to Google.

Google Posts and Q&A — Audit for recency and relevance. Unanswered Q&A questions are a missed opportunity to control the narrative around your business.

Services and Products — Fully filled-out service menus with keyword-rich descriptions improve GBP completeness signals.

Entity Consistency for AI Retrieval — AI systems like ChatGPT retrieve business information from structured data across multiple platforms. Your business name, category, service descriptions, and location must be consistent everywhere for AI engines to confidently recommend you.

Chapter 3: NAP Consistency and Citation Audit

Your canonical NAP is the single source of truth for your business identity. Every inconsistency across directories creates conflicting signals that weaken your local authority.

The audit process:

Define Your Canonical NAP — Decide on one exact format for your business name, address, and phone number. Document it. Every subsequent fix aligns to this standard.

Audit Tier 1 Directories — Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Maps, Facebook, Yelp, and your industry's primary directories. These carry the most authority weight.

Audit Tier 2 Directories — The top 50+ general directories: YellowPages, Foursquare, Manta, BBB, Angi, Houzz (for home services), and similar platforms.

Common Inconsistency Types to Flag:

  • Suite number included in some listings, omitted in others
  • Tracking phone numbers replacing the main business number
  • Abbreviations (St. vs Street, Ave. vs Avenue)
  • Old business names still active after a rebrand
  • Multiple GBP listings for the same location

Fix Workflow — Prioritize Tier 1 corrections manually. Use a citation management tool (BrightLocal, Whitespark, or Yext) for Tier 2 bulk corrections. Document every correction with before/after screenshots.

Chapter 4: Location Page and On-Page Audit

Your location page is the on-page signal that tells Google what you do, where you do it, and for whom. A weak location page limits your local pack eligibility regardless of how strong your GBP is.

Checklist:

Title Tag — Format: [Primary Service] in [City] | [Business Name]. Must include both the service keyword and the location modifier.

H1 — Should match or closely mirror the title tag. One H1 per page. No keyword stuffing.

NAP Embedded on Page — Your canonical NAP must appear on the page in crawlable text (not only inside an image or map embed). Place it in the footer and ideally within the body content.

LocalBusiness Schema — JSON-LD schema with @type: LocalBusiness (or the appropriate subtype), name, address, telephone, openingHours, geo, and url fields. Validate with Google's Rich Results Test.

Content Uniqueness — Each location page must have meaningfully unique content. Template-cloned pages with only the city name swapped create thin content issues across multi-location sites. Target a unique content ratio above 60%.

Internal Linking — Location pages should link to your primary service pages and receive links from your homepage and blog content. A location page with zero inbound internal links has weak page authority regardless of its content quality.

Mobile Performance — Google indexes the mobile version of your site. Run Core Web Vitals specifically for the mobile version of each location page.

Chapter 5: Review and Reputation Audit

Reviews now carry approximately 20% of local ranking weight — up from 16% in prior years. The audit goes beyond star rating.

What to measure:

Average Rating and Review Count — Baseline against your top three local competitors in Google Maps. If competitors have 200 reviews and you have 40, that gap is a ranking disadvantage.

Review Velocity — Recency matters. A business with 200 reviews, the most recent from 18 months ago, signals inactivity. Audit the date distribution of your reviews.

Response Rate and Quality — Both positive and negative reviews should receive responses. Generic responses ("Thanks for your kind words!") are less effective than specific, keyword-natural responses that acknowledge the service provided.

Platform Distribution — Review concentration on Google is strong, but spreading to industry-specific platforms (TripAdvisor for hospitality, Healthgrades for medical, Avvo for legal) strengthens your overall reputation footprint.

Review Spam and Fake Reviews — Flag any reviews that appear to violate Google's policies. Report clusters of negative reviews with similar language patterns.

Chapter 6: AI Search Visibility Audit

This is the audit dimension that every major local SEO audit guide misses — and it is increasingly the one that matters most for first-touch discovery.

The gap is stark: Google's Local 3-Pack shows a 35.9% appearance rate for local businesses. ChatGPT recommends only 1.2% of the 350,000+ business locations analyzed in SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index. If your business is not visible in AI search, you are invisible to a rapidly growing share of your potential customers.

How to Audit AI Search Visibility:

Run these prompt types in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews:

  • "[service] in [city]" — e.g., "plumber in Austin"
  • "best [service] near [zip code]" — e.g., "best accountant near 90210"
  • "[your business name] [city]" — direct brand mention test
  • "[service] [neighborhood]" — hyperlocal test

Document whether your business appears in the responses. Note which competitors are mentioned. This becomes your competitive baseline.

What Determines AI Recommendation:

AI systems retrieve local business information from structured data, authoritative directory listings, review content, and entity consistency across platforms. The factors most correlated with AI citation:

  • Consistent entity data across Google, Bing, Apple Maps, Yelp, and industry directories
  • High-quality FAQ content on your website that matches the natural language of AI queries
  • Schema markup that explicitly identifies your business type, services, and location
  • Review content that includes service-specific keywords (AI systems read reviews as evidence of what you do)

Chapter 7: Voice Search Readiness Audit

Voice searches are 3 to 5 times longer than typed searches and follow conversational question patterns that most location pages are not optimized for.

The audit checklist:

Question-Based Query Analysis — Identify the "who, what, where, when, why, how" questions your target customers ask. Tools: Google's People Also Ask, Answer The Public, and your own GBP Q&A section.

Featured Snippet Eligibility — Voice assistants predominantly read featured snippets aloud. Audit whether your location page or blog content ranks in position zero for any relevant local queries. If not, identify which answers your content could claim.

Conversational Keyword Gaps — Run a gap analysis between the long-tail, question-format queries your customers use and the formal keyword language your current pages target. These are rarely the same.

GBP Voice Answer Readiness — Google Assistant pulls business hours, directions, and basic service information directly from GBP for voice queries. Ensure your GBP is 100% complete, with accurate hours and a keyword-rich business description.

Chapter 8: Conversion and Engagement Audit

A local SEO audit that stops at rankings misses the entire point. Rankings drive visibility. Engagement drives revenue.

GBP provides engagement data that most businesses never look at:

GBP Engagement Metrics to Audit:

  • Direct searches (customers searching your business name) vs. discovery searches (customers finding you via category or service search)
  • Phone calls directly from GBP
  • Direction requests (strong purchase-intent signal)
  • Website clicks from GBP

Benchmark these metrics month over month. A drop in direction requests despite stable rankings often indicates a UX issue with your Maps listing (wrong address pin, missing photos, outdated hours).

Website Conversion Path Audit — Follow the path from a Maps click to a conversion action on your website. Audit load speed, mobile layout, contact form functionality, and call-to-action clarity. A website that loads slowly or fails on mobile loses a significant percentage of local traffic before it converts.

Call Tracking Verification — If you use call tracking numbers, audit that they are not replacing your canonical NAP number in directories. Tracking numbers break NAP consistency if deployed incorrectly.

Chapter 9: Service Area Business Audit Differences

Standard local SEO audit checklists assume a business with a physical address. Service area businesses — plumbers, electricians, cleaners, mobile services — need a completely different framework.

SAB-specific audit points:

GBP Verification Without Physical Address — SABs can hide their address on GBP and verify via video call instead. Audit that the service area is correctly configured, not just a hidden address.

`areaServed` Schema Instead of Address Schema — SABs should use areaServed in their LocalBusiness schema markup rather than a street address. Audit whether the schema accurately reflects your actual service coverage.

Service Area Page Quality — Each service area (city or neighborhood you serve) should have a dedicated landing page with unique content. Audit for thin content, duplicate template pages, and missing schema.

Geo-Grid Ranking for SABs — SABs need to rank across a geographic area, not just at a single point. Geo-grid analysis (see Chapter 11) is particularly important for SABs because ranking strength varies significantly across the service area.

Chapter 10: Multi-Location Audit Framework

A single-location audit approach applied to a multi-location business will miss the most common failure modes: NAP inconsistency at scale, location page duplication, and uneven review distribution.

Per-location NAP Verification — Audit each location independently. Common failures: corporate phone numbers appearing on all locations instead of local numbers, corporate address bleeding into individual location listings.

Location Page Uniqueness Scoring — Calculate the unique content ratio for each location page. Pages below 40% uniqueness relative to other location pages create thin content issues at scale.

GBP Verification Workflow for Franchises — Multi-location businesses need a documented GBP ownership and access protocol. Audit who owns each listing, whether ownership is centralized or distributed, and whether any locations have unclaimed or duplicate listings.

Review Distribution Audit — Map review counts by location. Locations with fewer than 30 reviews are ranking-disadvantaged relative to competitors regardless of other signals.

30/60/90-Day Rollout Roadmap — Multi-location audits produce large fix lists. Prioritize by impact: fix GBP errors first (30 days), then NAP corrections (60 days), then location page improvements (90 days).

Chapter 11: Geo-Grid Ranking Analysis

A geo-grid shows you where you actually rank across your service area — not just your average position. It reveals competitor dominance in specific neighborhoods and identifies coverage gaps your rankings report will never show.

What a Geo-Grid Is — A grid of pins (typically 5x5 or 7x7) placed across your target service area. Each pin represents a simulated search from that location. The result shows your rank at each point on the grid.

How to Interpret Results — Strong geo-grid performance looks like uniform high rankings across the entire grid. Common failure patterns: strong ranking near your physical address, weak ranking at the edges of your service area; competitor dominance in specific neighborhoods.

Competitor Benchmarking — Run the same geo-grid for your top three competitors. The overlay shows where you lead, where you trail, and which specific geographic areas represent growth opportunities.

Location Page Strategy from Geo-Grid Data — Neighborhoods where your geo-grid ranking is weak are the neighborhoods where you need dedicated service area pages. Geo-grid gaps translate directly into a content roadmap.

Tools: BrightLocal's Grid My Business, Local Falcon, or Whitespark's geo-grid feature.

Chapter 12: Industry-Specific Audit Additions

Generic audit checklists apply the same criteria to every business. Different industries have different local ranking factors, schema requirements, and review management constraints.

Restaurants — Audit menu schema (using hasMenu and MenuSection schema types), reservation links (OpenTable, Resy integration in GBP), cuisine type categories, and food-related photo volume. Google extracts menu data directly for AI summaries.

Healthcare — HIPAA compliance affects how you can respond to reviews (do not confirm or deny the reviewer is a patient). Audit Physician or MedicalClinic schema, insurance acceptance information in GBP, and HealthGrades/Zocdoc profile completeness.

Legal — Practice area pages each need independent local optimization. Audit Attorney schema, state bar admission information, and Avvo/Martindale profile completeness. Review response strategy must avoid creating implied attorney-client relationships.

Home Services — Audit licensed and insured badge display, emergency service hours in GBP, and ServiceTitan or similar dispatch tool integration with GBP booking. Angi and HomeAdvisor profile completeness matters for this vertical.

Real Estate — Audit MLS schema integration, RealEstateAgent schema, open house events in GBP, and neighborhood-specific location page structure.

Common Mistakes in Local SEO Audits

Mistake 1: Treating the GBP audit as the whole audit. GBP is one input. Ranking also depends on your website's on-page signals, your citation consistency, your review velocity, and your content's alignment with how AI systems retrieve local recommendations.

Mistake 2: Using tracking phone numbers in directories. Call tracking numbers break NAP consistency unless implemented with care. If you need call tracking, use it only on your website — not in GBP or directory listings.

Mistake 3: Auditing rankings without auditing conversions. A business ranking in position 1 of the Local 3-Pack with a 0.5% click-to-call rate has a conversion problem, not a rankings problem. Audit both.

Mistake 4: Skipping the AI visibility test. With 45% of consumers now using AI tools for local recommendations, ignoring ChatGPT and Perplexity visibility in your audit means ignoring a rapidly growing discovery channel.

Mistake 5: Applying a single-location checklist to multi-location businesses. Multi-location audits require per-location analysis. Aggregated data hides the location-specific failures that matter most.

FAQs

How much does a local SEO audit cost?

A basic local SEO audit covering GBP, citations, and on-page signals typically costs $300 to $800. A comprehensive audit covering all 7 dimensions — including AI visibility, geo-grid analysis, and conversion metrics — ranges from $800 to $2,500 depending on the number of locations and the depth of the deliverable.

How long does a local SEO audit take?

A single-location audit takes 3 to 5 business days for a thorough review. Multi-location audits scale with the number of locations — a 10-location audit typically requires 10 to 15 business days.

What is the difference between a local SEO audit and a website audit?

A website audit examines technical SEO, page speed, crawlability, and on-page elements across your full domain. A local SEO audit focuses specifically on local ranking factors: GBP, citations, reviews, location pages, and now AI search visibility. The two audits complement each other but address different ranking systems.

Can I do a local SEO audit myself?

Yes, partially. You can audit your GBP completeness, check NAP consistency across major directories, and run AI visibility prompts yourself. Geo-grid analysis, citation depth audits across 50+ directories, and technical schema validation typically require paid tools or a specialist.

How often should I run a local SEO audit?

A full audit quarterly is the standard recommendation for active local businesses. If you change your address, phone number, or business name — run an emergency audit immediately to catch NAP inconsistencies before they compound.

How do I audit AI search visibility?

Run your service plus city as a prompt in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews. Test 5 to 10 prompt variations. Document whether your business appears, which competitors appear instead, and what information the AI cites. This becomes your AI visibility baseline.

What if I do not have a physical address?

Service area businesses should hide their address on GBP, configure their service area correctly, use areaServed schema instead of address schema, and build dedicated service area landing pages for each city or neighborhood they serve.

What is a good local SEO score?

A useful local SEO "score" is competitive, not absolute. Your GBP completeness should match or exceed your top two competitors. Your review count and velocity should be at or above the local pack average for your category. Your NAP consistency should be 100% across Tier 1 directories.

Do I need a local SEO audit if I already rank in the top 3?

Yes. AI search visibility, voice search readiness, and conversion optimization are not captured in your current rankings. A business ranking in position 1 of the Local 3-Pack can still be invisible in ChatGPT and converting at a fraction of its potential.

What tools do I need for a local SEO audit?

Core tools: BrightLocal (citation audit + geo-grid), Google's Rich Results Test (schema validation), Google Search Console (indexing and on-page data), and manual testing in ChatGPT and Perplexity (AI visibility). Optional: Local Falcon or Whitespark for geo-grid analysis.

Conclusion

A local SEO audit in 2026 is not a GBP checklist. It is a seven-dimension analysis that covers how your business performs across traditional search, AI-powered search, voice, and conversion — for your specific business type, whether you have one location or fifty.

The businesses pulling ahead in local search right now are not the ones with the most citations. They are the ones that understand how AI engines select local recommendations, have entity data consistent enough for AI systems to trust, and have location pages designed to convert the traffic they earn.
