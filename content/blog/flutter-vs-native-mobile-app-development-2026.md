## Quick Takeaways

- Flutter ships to both iOS and Android from **one codebase**.
- Native is suitable for hardware-intensive applications.
- The right choice depends on **app complexity, timeline, budget, and team skills**.

## Introduction

Every founder building a mobile app hits the same wall: **Flutter or native?**

It sounds like a technical question. It's actually a **business decision** that affects:

- Development cost
- Time to market
- Maintenance
- Performance
- Team requirements

## The Problem: Why Mobile Stack Choice Matters in 2026

### 1. Flutter Has Evolved

Flutter now provides high-performance rendering and can support production applications across iOS and Android.

### 2. Native Development Requires Separate Platforms

Native development generally means separate iOS and Android codebases, which can increase development and maintenance requirements.

### 3. User Expectations Have Changed

Users expect:

- Fast app loading
- Smooth scrolling
- Offline functionality where needed
- Efficient battery usage

## Why This Problem Happens

### Root Cause 1: The Framework Debate Is Framed Wrong

Performance is only one part of the decision. Cost, timeline, complexity, and team skills also matter.

### Root Cause 2: Cost Differences

Native development can require separate iOS and Android development resources, while Flutter allows both platforms to be developed from one codebase.

## Possible Solutions: Four Stacks Compared

| Factor | Flutter | Native iOS + Android | React Native |
|---|---|---|---|
| Codebase | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| Language | Dart | Swift + Kotlin | JavaScript/TypeScript |
| Cost | $15k–$30k | $40k–$70k | $18k–$35k |
| Timeline | 8–12 weeks | 16–20 weeks | 9–13 weeks |

### Solution 1: Flutter

**Pros:** One codebase, faster development, consistent UI.

**Cons:** Larger app size and some features may require native plugins.

**Best for:** MVPs, SaaS, e-commerce, social, productivity, and content apps.

### Solution 2: Native

**Pros:** Maximum platform performance and hardware access.

**Cons:** Separate development and maintenance for iOS and Android.

**Best for:** 3D games, AR/VR, and applications requiring extensive hardware integration.

### Solution 3: React Native

**Pros:** JavaScript/TypeScript ecosystem and strong web development alignment.

**Cons:** Some platform-specific UI and performance considerations.

**Best for:** Teams with existing React experience.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Choosing a Stack Without Considering Requirements

The technology should match the application's actual requirements.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring Maintenance

Consider ongoing maintenance, updates, testing, and platform changes when calculating total cost.

### Mistake 3: Choosing Based Only on Price

The cheapest initial quote may not represent the lowest total cost over the life of the application.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a mobile app cost in 2026?

A basic MVP can range from **$15k–$35k with Flutter** and **$40k–$70k with native development**, depending on scope and requirements.

### Can Flutter apps access native hardware features?

Yes. Flutter can integrate with native platform functionality through plugins and native code when required.

### How long does it take to build a mobile app?

An MVP can take several weeks to several months depending on complexity, features, team size, and platform requirements.

## Conclusion

Choosing between Flutter and native development depends on your application's requirements, budget, timeline, performance needs, and available development expertise.
